Split Math

The tonnage on the box was never told about the knot, the cycle time on the sticker was measured with an empty ram, and the horsepower was never asked where the amps come from.

Open the app →

The tonnage truth

A round wants tons in proportion to its inches, and the wood has opinions: seasoned pine asks about half a ton an inch, oak a full one, green knotty rounds half again. Held against that, the 22-ton box either covers the round or stalls on the tough ones.

The cycle truth

The sticker cycle is the empty ram gliding out and back. Under load the honest cycle runs about 1.4 times that, and the rounds-per-hour pace drops to what the wood actually allows.

The circuit truth

An electric splitter drinks from a wall outlet. After the continuous-load derate, the 15-amp circuit honestly feeds about 1.9 horsepower - so the 2-horsepower sticker is counting the startup surge, and the 5-horsepower one is marketing first.

What it never does

It never calls the empty stroke a work pace, never confuses the surge with the running draw, and never forgets the norms are labeled - the maul keeps the honest ledger.

Split Math is open source - the engine is small enough to read, and the math is plain enough to argue with.
